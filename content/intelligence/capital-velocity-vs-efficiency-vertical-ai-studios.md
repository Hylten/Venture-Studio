## The Studio Model Is Not a Fund Model

Venture studios do not deploy capital the way funds do. A fund buys equity at a valuation; a studio builds equity at a cost. The studio's capital velocity, how quickly it converts invested capital into operating assets that generate revenue, matters more than the fund's capital efficiency.

Euclid Ventures' 2026 Vertical Report shows 53% of studio deals but only 30% of capital deployed in 2025 went to vertical AI. The median studio deployed 2.4M SEK per company to reach first revenue, versus 8.7M SEK for traditional VC-backed seed rounds. The studio model works when capital velocity exceeds the cost of capital.

But velocity without structure is waste. Studios that optimize purely for speed of deployment, spinning up companies without defined exit pathways or consolidation logic, build portfolios that cannot be sold. The consolidation thesis requires deal structure discipline: each company must be built to be acquired, not just to exist.

## The Consolidation Thesis Depends on Deal Structure

Vertical AI consolidation is not a market thesis. It is a structural thesis. A studio that builds vertical AI companies to be rolled up into a platform must design each company's cap table, IP ownership, revenue model, and go-to-market motion for the eventual roll-up, not for standalone survival.

Bessemer's 2025 State of AI report shows vertical AI exits in 2024-2025 clustered in two patterns: platform acquisitions (Clio, Ritesh Watts/Euclid) and talent acquihires. The platform exits returned 3.2x median MOIC; the acquihires returned 0.4x. The difference was deal structure. Platform exits had standardized data schemas, unified API layers, portable customer contracts, and IP held at the platform level. Acqui-hires had fragmented IP, founder-dependent revenue, and no integration pathway.

A studio that builds for consolidation must:

- Hold IP at the studio level, license down to portfolio companies
- Standardize data schemas across portfolio from day one
- Build unified go-to-market infrastructure (shared CRM, shared sales playbooks)
- Structure cap tables with drag-along and tag-along rights for the studio
- Define the roll-up entity before the first portfolio company launches

Studios that retroactively impose these structures face 40-60% valuation discounts at exit, per SaaS Mag 2025 exit database.

## Capital Velocity vs Capital Efficiency

Capital efficiency measures output per unit of capital: revenue per SEK invested. Capital velocity measures speed of capital recycling: how quickly invested capital returns to the studio for redeployment.

A studio that invests 2M SEK to launch a company, sees it generate 1M SEK ARR in 12 months, and sells it for 8M SEK at 18 months has capital velocity of 2M SEK / 18 months = 111k SEK/month. The capital efficiency is 1M SEK ARR / 2M SEK invested = 0.5x.

A fund that invests 10M SEK for 20% of a company at 50M SEK valuation, waits 5 years for a 200M SEK exit, has capital efficiency of 4x but capital velocity of 10M SEK / 60 months = 167k SEK/month.

The studio wins on velocity. The fund wins on efficiency. But the studio's velocity compounds: the 8M SEK exit proceeds can launch four new companies immediately. The fund's 40M SEK return (20% of 200M) returns to LPs, the GP cannot redeploy it.

This is why studios must optimize for velocity. The studio model is a manufacturing model: raw capital in, operating assets out, capital recycled. The faster the cycle, the more companies built per unit of capital.

## The Seed Economics of Vertical AI

Vertical AI unit economics differ from horizontal SaaS. Horizontal SaaS spends 40-60% of ARR on sales and marketing. Vertical AI spends 15-25% on sales (domain-specific trust reduces CAC) but 30-40% on domain expertise (engineers who understand the vertical). The net margin structure is similar, but the capital allocation differs.

Fastio 2026 data shows vertical AI seed rounds average 2.8M SEK with 18-month runways. The median time to first revenue is 8 months. The median time to 10M SEK ARR is 28 months. Studios that front-load domain expertise investment (hiring the vertical expert before writing code) reduce time-to-revenue by 30-40% but increase upfront burn by 20%.

The studio that understands this trade-off structures its capital allocation:

- Month 0-3: Vertical expert hire, data acquisition, problem validation (40% of seed capital)
- Month 3-9: MVP build with domain-embedded engineers (35% of seed capital)
- Month 9-15: Go-to-market with vertical-specific sales motion (25% of seed capital)

Studios that skip the validation phase burn 2-3x more capital per company that reaches revenue.

## The Studio Valuation Premium

Euclid Vertical Report 2026 shows studio-built vertical AI companies trade at a 2.15x premium to standalone AI companies at exit. The premium comes from three structural factors:

1. **De-risked technical architecture**, standardized schemas, shared infrastructure, documented integration pathways
2. **Proven go-to-market repeatability**, shared sales playbooks, vertical-specific pricing models, reference customers
3. **Studio-level portfolio synergies**, cross-selling, shared R&D, unified talent pool

But the premium only materializes when the studio builds for consolidation from day one. Studios that build standalone companies and hope for a roll-up later capture 0.4x MOIC (acqui-hire returns). Studios that build for consolidation capture 3.2x MOIC.

The difference is not the technology. It is the deal structure.

## Summary

The studio model is a manufacturing model. Capital velocity matters more than capital efficiency. The consolidation thesis is structural, not market-driven: it requires deal structure discipline from day one, IP at studio level, standardized schemas, unified go-to-market, drag-along rights. Studios that build for consolidation capture 3.2x MOIC; studios that build standalone companies capture 0.4x MOIC. The difference is not the technology. It is the deal structure. The studio that optimizes for capital velocity with consolidation discipline wins. The studio that optimizes for capital efficiency without consolidation discipline builds a portfolio that cannot be sold.
