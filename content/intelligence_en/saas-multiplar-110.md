## Introduction: Multiples Move Ahead of Narratives

In the SaaS sector, multiples always lead. They move before revenues, before adoption, before regulatory dust has settled, and long before operational realities have caught up. The market is not a judge of the present but a discounting machine for future dominance. Yet 2026 is an exception in one respect: multiple expansion and multiple contraction are now driven less by macro factors and more by a sharp structural difference between two categories of companies. One category absorbs capital, talent, and narratives. The other is left in a technological backwater where pricing, margins, and integrations gradually lose strategic value.

What was previously a narrow vertical within data platforms has become the main artery of the investment system: AGI infrastructure. Not as slogans, but as actual system-level dependencies. This shift has two effects: multiples on infrastructure companies are pulled up by an expected oligopolization, while multiples on application layers are pushed down by increasing substitutability, margin compression, and a predictable consolidation under a few horizontal services.

It is not the market that suddenly undervalues applications. It is the infrastructure that has become the rational place for multiple expansion.

## Why AGI Infrastructure Drives the Narrative

Infrastructure companies are in the same cycle that hyperscalers were in 2014, but with a higher ceiling and faster technical shifts. These are companies that push the boundaries of what is possible in model capacity, training pipeline efficiency, data engines, modifiable runtime environments, security layers around inference, and the orchestration of distributed compute clusters. The market does not interpret this as research or innovation in itself, but as the establishment of a new baseline for all applications that follow.

The result is that everything built on top of the infrastructure is judged on its acceleration and leverage relative to these platforms. If applications are perceived as thin wrappers around generic models, the multiple compression is logical: the market is not pricing functionality, but irreplaceability and production criticality.

AGI infrastructure companies have the inherent advantage that their customer value is not endpoints but pipelines. Each new customer integration strengthens their moat rather than increasing their churn risk. This contrasts with SaaS applications where a significant portion of innovation is imitative and where functional differentiation is quickly consumed by the model itself. The market responds accordingly.

## Systemic Shifts Behind Multiple Divergence

To understand why multiples diverge so sharply between layers, one must consider the reinforcing interactions of system layers. The three shifts affecting 2026’s valuation logic are:

1. The emergence of compute monopolies
2. The absorption of application value by models
3. The return of data systems as a competitive advantage

These three forces create a structural reality where infrastructure is perceived as primary value, and applications as derivatives.

## The Compute Monopoly

Despite the rhetoric about openness, the compute economy is not evenly distributed. Common arguments that GPU access will democratize are contradicted by actual capacity outcomes. 2025 and 2026 have been characterized by block allocations, long-term capacity contracts, and a growing gap between actors with capital-rich partnerships and everyone else.

In this reality, AGI infrastructure companies become more like energy producers than software vendors. They own access to the raw material that every application needs: compute capacity combined with model pipeline control. The market sees this as a precursor to oligopoly, which historically justifies multiples far above traditional SaaS valuation models.

## The Absorption of Application Value by Models

From late 2024 onward, models began solving problems that previously resided at the application layer: contextual understanding, multi-step logic, domain restrictions, real-time integration, data transformation, semantic search, and pipeline generation. This means that applications are increasingly reduced to workflow configurations.

When model capabilities accelerate faster than application rollout cycles, the market’s rational conclusion is that the application’s value is not enduring. A functional advantage becomes a short-lived arbitrage position. Multiples that previously rewarded gross margins and recurring revenue now move toward rewarding technical moat-creating capacity—something the application layer struggles to demonstrate.

## The Return of Data Systems

Data systems that were previously considered limited to analytical processing have gained a new role in agent pipelines. In 2026, data quality, data availability, and data mobility are central to inference quality and model optimization. It is no longer a question of dashboards and ETL, but of actively influencing the decision space of models.

This means that infrastructure-adjacent systems capable of handling continuous data input, model versioning, situational context, and real-time response gain a strategic role that cannot be replicated by applications without building parallel data stacks. This capital intensity makes the market interpret infrastructure as the stable part of the value chain and applications as the volatile part.

## The Application Layer’s Multiple Crisis Is Structural, Not Cyclical

When multiples are compressed, the explanation is usually macro. But 2026’s multiple compression in the application layer has nothing to do with interest rates or liquidity. It is a consequence of applications historically being valued as if their functional layer was harder to copy than it is in a model-centered world.

There are three main reasons for this:

1. Features have become language. Previously, features required code; now they require prompt structures and model configurations.

2. Integration costs have dropped dramatically. The application’s defensibility was partly built on the hassle of switching systems. When AGI infrastructure offers abstraction layers that make integrations generic, this line of defense disappears.

3. Margins are correcting. The gross margins that were considered protected in SaaS have begun to erode as inference costs hit the application’s COGS, while customers expect faster iteration without willingness to pay more.

This makes the market not see the multiple compression as a temporary effect. It is seen as a new baseline.

## Why Infrastructure Receives a Valuation Premium

The market’s logic right now is simple: invest where value is cumulative, not where value is consumable. The infrastructure layer generates cumulative value through four mechanisms:

1. Scale advantages in compute increase with each additional customer, as pipeline optimization and model monitoring improve.

2. Defensibility increases as models’ competence depends on training data, something that cannot be imitated without extreme cost.

3. Network effects in data input create improvements that spread across the entire customer base.

4. Regulatory thresholds benefit from spillover effects where compliance at the infrastructure level covers the application layer as a whole.

Application layers have none of these four mechanisms in their structure. Their value is linear with each new customer rather than exponential. That is why multiples diverge.

## How Venture Studios Read Market Signals in 2026

Builders of systems rather than products have a strategic advantage in the current landscape. When multiples shift from applications to infrastructure, what counts as an early promising signal also changes. Whereas traditional SaaS studios previously sought:

- early customer adoption
- high usage frequency
- low churn numbers
- clear ICP profiles

system-oriented studios today look for entirely different parameters:

- model feedback loops with increasing marginal utility
- data quality that improves model performance across the stack
- robust runtime environments capable of carrying agent-based systems
- capacity to integrate with multiple compute paradigms

This type of studio logic is one reason why more capital flows are moving toward infrastructure. It is simply easier to build highly valued systems when valuation logic follows the system’s depth rather than its surface.

## SaaS as a Category Needs a New Definition

SaaS in 2026 is not a monolithic category. It is rather three different subcategories with entirely different multiple logics:

1. Functional applications
2. Domain-specific agent layers
3. Infrastructure-driven intelligent systems

The market’s multiples reflect these differences rather than treating SaaS as a homogeneous asset class. Functional applications are compressed. Agent layers are compressed unless they can absorb data at the right scale. Infrastructure-driven systems receive an expansion premium.

What counts as SaaS has therefore become a spectrum rather than a category.

## Why Application Layers Still Don’t Disappear

Despite multiple compression, the application layer is not irrelevant. It has simply taken on a new role. Applications function as:

- control layers
- orchestration interfaces
- specialized policy engines
- security filters
- context encapsulation for specific workflows

But the market no longer prices this role as highly as before, because it is considered replaceable. The application’s function is still central, but its value is fragmented and movable. Therefore, it does not receive the same multiples.

## Capital Movement and the Psychology of Multiples

Every major shift in multiples follows the same psychological curve:

1. Denial: the market assumes application margins will return.
2. Acceptance: the market understands that value absorption is occurring in the model and data layers.
3. Acceleration: capital is allocated aggressively to infrastructure.
4. Overreaction: application layers are underinvested even when certain verticals show strong moat.
5. Consolidation: the few applications built around proprietary data begin to regain multiple space.

2026 is between points two and three. The market’s conviction that future value capture occurs in AGI infrastructure is now explicit, not implicit. That is why multiples are moving so fast.

## Why Multiples Are Expected to Diverge Even Further in 2026 and 2027

The discounting logic is simple: if infrastructure consolidates into 3 to 5 global players, these will gain pricing power that exceeds what SaaS players have historically had. This makes them attractive, not despite capital intensity, but because of it. We have seen similar patterns in telecom, cloud, and semiconductors. Every sector where technological thresholds are so high that only a few players can survive tends to receive multiples above traditional valuation models.

Application layers, on the other hand, risk moving toward a model where most players become marginal products tied to the same infrastructure providers. This makes the market price them as structurally dependent rather than structurally foundational.

## Where We Go from Here

What remains to be analyzed is how venture studios, larger SaaS companies, and infrastructure platforms will build around this multiple structure. Especially important is understanding:

- how agent ecosystems change the value chain
- how data lock-in becomes a new moat
- how application layers can create defensibility despite the models’ absorption power
- what architectures will define the next generation of intelligent systems

These questions are central to understanding how valuation logic evolves further and what strategic choices are required to build systems that not only survive in this environment but take a structural leadership position.

## The Pull of Infrastructure in an Era of Cognitive Asymmetry

When market multiples shift, it is rarely the result of a single event. It occurs when a sufficient number of investors accept a new map of the future and begin optimizing for it. During the period 2024 to 2026, one of the fastest such redrawings since the mobile revolution occurred. Multiples for SaaS applications fell while valuations for companies in AGI infrastructure rose. This was not a coincidence. It was a reaction to the market understanding that cognitive asymmetry now arises between those building on raw intelligence capacity and those sitting on top in the application layer.

Cognitive asymmetry means that some players have access to more powerful, faster, and cheaper models than others. It is a state that creates an advantage because intelligence is no longer more expensive to scale than running a traditional server. When this became clear to capital, the application layer became a direct risk. Ecosystems where the marginal cost of building a new application approaches zero tend to drive multiples downward because barriers to entry disappear. At the same time, value flows upward in the stack toward those sitting on the raw material of intelligence, or on the software, hardware, and infrastructure required to produce it.

That is why 2026 became the year the market accepted that AGI infrastructure is not just a product category but a new fundamental industrial resource. Investors stopped asking which specific application would win and began asking which layers were impossible to replace.

## Why AGI Infrastructure Captures Multiples

There are three main reasons why infrastructure companies captured capital.

First, they control the pipeline for training, inference, and optimization of large models. This resembles how semiconductor companies in previous eras took more of the value chain as process technology advanced. Those who own the raw material of intelligence production can dictate terms upward in the stack.

Second, economies of scale in AGI infrastructure are extremely strong. When the cost per trained token drops and optimized inference clusters deliver exponential productivity gains, it becomes practically impossible for smaller players to compete without significant capital. This means the market expects high concentration, which drives valuations upward.

Third, investors perceive infrastructure companies as being closest to what since 2025 has been considered the next major paradigm shift: the transition from LLM economy to cognitive economy, where models no longer function as generative tools but as autonomous problem solvers. This transition makes the infrastructure’s role equivalent to that of electricity, internet bandwidth, and GPU-accelerated compute power in previous eras.

This psychological shift directly affects multiples. The market no longer pays for user growth in applications but for control over the production capacity of artificial cognition.

## The Paradox of the Application Layer

SaaS companies previously had a self-evident value discussion: recurring revenue, low churn, high gross margins, and relatively stable demand. In 2026, the logic is different. There are three paradoxical phenomena pressing multiples.

The first is that users are no longer humans. When AI-based agents account for a large share of interactions in applications, user growth is not proof of demand. It is proof of automation. This erases traditional key metrics and makes it difficult to ever argue that the number of users alone justifies high multiples.

The second is that product differentiation is decreasing. Applications that previously required 20 engineers over two years can in 2026 be replicated by a single developer with access to an advanced agent chain. The cost of copying is so close to zero that the market no longer believes in lasting moat in application companies. This is a psychological shift as much as a technological one.

The third is that the application layer risks becoming a value layer for the model itself. As models become more contextually aware, they can generate complete applications, including UI, API integrations, and logic. This makes investors see the application layer as a consumption layer rather than a product layer. The result is multiple compression.

The market is therefore not actively choosing to abandon the application layer. It is simply observing that the long-term value distribution has shifted in favor of infrastructure.

## The Cost Revolution Driving Multiples

The most underestimated shift between 2024 and 2026 is not the capability of intelligence but the cost curve for producing it. This cost curve affects multiples in two ways.

First, when the cost of advanced inference drops sharply, the perception of value shifts to those who can scale inference extremely cheaply. This applies not only to cloud companies but also to those who have succeeded in taking control of everything from chip design to training techniques such as quantization, sparsity, and compressed training cycles. These companies are seen as part of a new fossil-free intelligence grid, where energy use, cooling, and optimization are linked to model performance.

Second, the application layer no longer has any cost advantage left. In previous technology waves, application companies could compete through efficiency and speed. In the cognitive economy, speed is democratized and efficiency is a function of access to cheap intelligence rather than organizational excellence. This creates a psychological upheaval: investors no longer focus on how fast a team can build but on what the team has exclusive control over.

The cost curve is therefore a driver of multiples both directly through gross margins and indirectly through how investors perceive the scale of technology.

## The Three New Moats Defining AGI Infrastructure Companies’ Valuations

In an environment where intelligence has become the primary raw material, investors begin identifying three new moats that determine which companies deserve high multiples.

The first is lock-in through data ownership and data quality. Even if models can be improved through generative self-training, high-quality, curated, and domain-specific data is still required for critical applications. Infrastructure companies sitting on this data have a long-term advantage that application companies cannot match.

The second is integrated hardware and software stack. When training and inference are deeply dependent on accelerators, network topologies, and smart caching, the value of vertical integration becomes enormous. Companies that can optimize the entire pipeline chain have an advantage similar to what Apple had when they transitioned from Intel to their own chips.

The third is regulatory symbiosis. AGI infrastructure companies are in many countries considered critical infrastructure. This means that states engage in collaboration rather than competition. This creates a market advantage where the provider becomes the standard rather than an option.

These three moats are difficult to break. They are also easy to communicate to the market, further reinforcing multiples through the power of narrative.

## The Psychology Behind Capital’s Rebalancing

Capital markets are more narrative-driven than they care to admit. When a new technological wave emerges, it is less about technical facts and more about which future vision investors accept as most likely. From late 2025 to mid-2026, the narrative of the application layer’s vulnerability and the inevitability of infrastructure value became dominant.

What made this rebalancing faster than previous shifts was that no one could ignore the speed of development. Application companies that were market leaders in 2024 had their core functions commoditized by agent-based auto-engineering by 2026. This created a sense that even large companies could fall overnight.

Infrastructure companies, on the other hand, were perceived as stable, capital-intensive, and politically prioritized. Their competitive advantages were seen as more robust than a new interface or a workflow improvement. The market reacted by awarding these companies multiples previously reserved for SaaS companies with explosive growth.

The psychology behind the shift is therefore both fear and attraction. Fear of the application layer being too easy to disrupt and attraction to the robustness of infrastructure.

## What Is Happening in 2026 Is the Beginning of a Longer Cycle

It is important to understand that the multiple shift is not temporary. It is the beginning of a period where intelligence is seen as a raw material and where control over the production of this raw material becomes central to the global economy.

The application layer will continue to generate revenue and create innovation, but capital has already chosen where value will accumulate. That is why we continue to see that companies built on top of AGI are short-lived while companies at the core of infrastructure become long-lived and are valued accordingly.

The psychological drivers behind this development are rational in a world where intelligence production is the primary symbol of competitiveness. And that is why 2026 is the year the market stopped comparing SaaS to previous software generations and instead began seeing AGI infrastructure as the next global base layer for the economy.
